The problem

The AI attack surface is growing faster than security teams can track.

Every agent a developer ships can reach databases, email, repositories, and internal APIs. Data protection that works at rest and in transit stops the moment a model reads it. The controls built for the last era weren't designed for software that decides for itself.

Shadow AI

Employees paste sensitive data into unapproved AI tools, and nobody finds out until after the incident.

Ungoverned agents

Agents ship without security review, with broad tool access and no registered owner.

Poisoned tooling

Malicious MCP servers and tampered tool definitions can redirect an agent toward exfiltration.

No proof

When the auditor asks what a model saw and what an agent did, most organizations have no record to show.

John has spent three decades running technology where the stakes are highest. Today he writes the code himself.

He was Chief Technology Officer at News Corp, AOL, Merrill Lynch, and GE Capital, leading engineering organizations of thousands. At Merrill Lynch, his team delivered the firm's first robo-advisor in 2002. He began his career at Ernst & Young, where he became a partner and helped establish the firm's US computer security practice.

Since 2008 he has served on the Equifax board, where he chairs the Technology Committee and oversees AI security, AI in software engineering, and the company's cloud transformation. Through Great Falls Ventures he has backed AI companies since 2007, starting with Powerset (acquired by Microsoft and built into Bing).

He founded McKinley Labs in 2025 to build the security infrastructure he wanted as a board member and couldn't buy.
